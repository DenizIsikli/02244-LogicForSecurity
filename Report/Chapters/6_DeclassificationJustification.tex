\section{Declassification and Principal Authority}

Declassification (controlled downgrading of labels) is critical for medical research, anonymization, and multi-stakeholder collaboration. This section justifies declassification as security mechanism (not violation), specifies formal conditions, and demonstrates the hospital scenario with audit trails.

\subsection{Principles and Authorization Framework}

In Myers-Liskov, declassification is legitimate when authorized by data owners. Principal $p$ can declassify information labeled $p: R_1$ to $p: R_2$ (where $R_2 \supseteq R_1$) because $p$ controls policies. This is NOT a security violation—it's legitimate ownership right.

\textbf{Rationale}: 
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Owner Principal Controls Policies}: Each principal defines who can read their data. Owner $p$ has ultimate authority over $p$-labeled data.
\item \textbf{Monotonic Reader Expansion}: Declassification only adds readers ($R' \supseteq R$). No readers removed. Conservative expansion maintains existing confidentiality for authorized parties.
\item \textbf{No Unauthorized Access}: Only owner can trigger declassification. Unauthorized principals cannot gain access. Owner explicitly chooses to extend access.
\item \textbf{Fully Auditable}: All declassifications logged with timestamp, principal, justification, signature. Enables post-hoc review and breach investigation.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Contrast with Centralized Models}: Centralized systems (e.g., Volpano) require central administrator approval for all label changes. This creates bottlenecks, scalability issues, and denies data owners autonomy. Myers-Liskov distributes declassification authority to principals, enabling decentralized decision-making without administrator overhead.

\subsection{Formal Declassification Conditions}

Declassification must satisfy formal conditions to be justified. Three conditions support the hospital medical research scenario:

\subsubsection{Condition 1: K-Anonymity for De-identification}

De-identification of patient records requires k-anonymity protection.

\textbf{Definition}: A dataset is k-anonymous if each quasi-identifier value combination (e.g., age + zip code + gender) appears at least $k$ times. Re-identification risk is bounded: attacker with external knowledge matching $\geq k$ records cannot uniquely identify individual.

\textbf{Mathematical Specification}: For dataset $D$ with quasi-identifiers $Q = \{q_1, \ldots, q_m\}$:
\begin{equation}
\forall v \in \text{domain}(Q), |\{r \in D : r[Q] = v\}| \geq k
\end{equation}

\textbf{Hospital Standard}: $k = 5$ ensures re-identification risk $\leq 20\%$ per patient record per HIPAA guidance. With k=5, attacker knows quasi-identifier but matches 5+ records; probability identifying correct record $\leq 1/5 = 20\%$.

\textbf{Declassification Justification}: If dataset provably k-anonymous, then data no longer personally identifiable. Records now researcher property (ownership transferred). Expansion to researcher readers justified by technical de-identification guarantee.

\subsubsection{Condition 2: Aggregation for Statistical Release}

Aggregate statistics (means, counts, percentiles) computed from groups $\geq k$ individuals are inherently non-sensitive.

\textbf{Definition}: Aggregate statistics are non-sensitive if they combine data from $\geq k$ individuals and no individual record recoverable.

\textbf{Mathematical Specification}: If count-based aggregates (sum, mean, median) computed from record groups $\geq k$, individual values not recoverable from aggregate alone. Example: mean = (patient1.age + patient2.age + patient3.age + patient4.age + patient5.age)/5. Individual ages hidden; only population mean observable.

\textbf{Examples}:
\begin{itemize}
\item Mean tumor size from 5+ patients: not sensitive. Aggregate only, individuals hidden.
\item Median patient age: not sensitive. Statistical summary independent of individual values.
\item Count of anomalies: not sensitive if count $\geq 5$ patients. Aggregate only.
\item Percentile distributions: not sensitive if computed from groups $\geq k$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contrast}: If statistics computed from individual patients (e.g., single patient's measurements), then sensitive and cannot be declassified to public.

\textbf{Declassification Justification}: Aggregate statistics inherently non-personal. Declassification to public readers justified by mathematical property (aggregates non-recoverable).

\subsubsection{Condition 3: Differential Privacy for Query Responses}

Advanced declassification supports differential privacy: aggregate query responses that don't enable individual record reconstruction.

\textbf{Definition}: Query response satisfies $\epsilon$-differential privacy if response distribution is indistinguishable for databases differing in single record. Attacker cannot determine if specific patient included in dataset by observing query response.

\textbf{Specification}: Response probability $P(\text{response} | \text{database})$ and $P(\text{response} | \text{database}')$ (where database' differs from database by one record) satisfy:
\begin{equation}
P(\text{response} | D) \leq e^\epsilon \cdot P(\text{response} | D')
\end{equation}

\textbf{Practical Application}: Hospital releases anonymized cohort statistics ("study included 100+ patients, mean age X, median tumor size Y") with $\epsilon = 0.5$ (strong privacy). External observer cannot distinguish whether specific patient included.

\textbf{Declassification Justification}: Differential privacy mathematically guarantees individual records not identifiable from query responses. Declassification to public justified by formal privacy proof.

\subsection{Hospital Medical Study Scenario: Complete Walkthrough}

Three-step declassification workflow: Patient Consent → De-identification → Publication.

\textbf{Step 1 - Patient Consent and Authorization}

Patient $P_1$ uploads medical data (cancer diagnosis, imaging, treatment plan):
\begin{verbatim}
UPLOAD(
  data = {diagnosis: "Stage 2", imaging: [CT scans], treatment: "Chemo+Radiation"},
  principal = patient_1,
  readers = {doctor_1, patient_1}
)
\end{verbatim}

Label: $L_0 = \text{patient}_1: \{\text{doctor}_1, \text{patient}_1\}$. Only patient and treating doctor can read.

Patient $P_1$ receives research recruitment from analyzer (AI system for cancer detection):
\begin{verbatim}
DECLASSIFY(
  data_id = P1_data,
  new_readers = {doctor_1, patient_1, analyzer},
  justification = "Authorize medical AI analysis for cancer research study",
  authorizing_principal = patient_1
)
\end{verbatim}

Check: authorizing\_principal (patient\_1) = owner (patient\_1) ✓ Approved

New label: $L_1 = \text{patient}_1: \{\text{doctor}_1, \text{patient}_1, \text{analyzer}\}$

Audit log entry:
\begin{verbatim}
[timestamp: 2026-04-20 15:04:26]
principal: patient_1
action: DECLASSIFY
old_label: patient_1:{doctor_1, patient_1}
new_label: patient_1:{doctor_1, patient_1, analyzer}
justification: "Authorize medical AI analysis for cancer research study"
signature: SHA256(entry)
\end{verbatim}

\textbf{Step 2 - De-identification and Analyzer Declassification}

Analyzer processes data: extracts features, runs de-identification algorithm (k-anonymity with k=5), removes direct identifiers (name, MRN, specific dates), retains quasi-identifiers (age, zip code, gender, cancer type, stage).

Analyzer output (anonymized dataset): 50 patients from same hospital, all with cancer diagnoses. Each quasi-identifier combination (age, zip, gender, stage) appears $\geq 5$ times. Re-identification risk $\leq 20\%$.

Analyzer writes dataset; declares ownership transfer to researcher principal:
\begin{verbatim}
DECLASSIFY(
  data_id = anonymized_cohort,
  new_readers = {public},
  justification = "K-anonymity (k=5) satisfied: QID combinations appear >=5 times, 
                   re-id risk <=20%. Suitable for sharing with research community.",
  authorizing_principal = analyzer
)
\end{verbatim}

Check: authorizing\_principal (analyzer) = owner (analyzer, who created anonymized\_cohort) ✓ Approved

New label: $L_2 = \text{researcher}: \{\text{researcher}, \text{statistician}, \text{public}\}$ (ownership transferred; readers include public)

Audit log entry:
\begin{verbatim}
[timestamp: 2026-04-20 15:05:00]
principal: analyzer
action: DECLASSIFY
old_label: analyzer:{}
new_label: researcher:{researcher, statistician, public}
justification: "K-anonymity (k=5) satisfied..."
k_anonymity_report: "QID=[age,zip,gender,stage]; all combinations: n>=5; re-id_risk<=0.2"
signature: SHA256(entry)
\end{verbatim}

\textbf{Step 3 - Aggregate Statistics and Publication}

Research team (statisticians) computes aggregate statistics from anonymized cohort:
\begin{itemize}
\item Mean age: 58.3 years (from 50+ patients)
\item Median tumor size: 4.2 cm (from 50+ patients)
\item Count by stage: Stage 1 (n=15), Stage 2 (n=22), Stage 3 (n=13) [all n >= 5]
\item Survival rate: 92\% (from 50+ patients)
\end{itemize}

Researchers publish in medical journal. Final declassification:
\begin{verbatim}
DECLASSIFY(
  data_id = aggregates,
  new_readers = {public},
  justification = "Aggregate statistics only, no individual records. Computed from
                   cohort n>=5. Statistics non-recoverable to individuals.",
  authorizing_principal = researcher
)
\end{verbatim}

Check: authorizing\_principal (researcher) = owner (researcher) ✓ Approved

New label: $L_3 = \text{researcher}: \{\text{public}\}$ (published; everyone can read)

Audit log entry:
\begin{verbatim}
[timestamp: 2026-04-20 15:06:00]
principal: researcher
action: DECLASSIFY
old_label: researcher:{researcher, statistician}
new_label: researcher:{public}
justification: "Aggregate statistics only..."
conditions_satisfied: [aggregation_threshold=50_patients, individual_records_redacted=true]
signature: SHA256(entry)
\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Audit Trail and Accountability}

All declassifications form audit trail:

\begin{tabular}{|l|l|l|l|}
\hline
\textbf{Step} & \textbf{Principal} & \textbf{Condition} & \textbf{New Readers} \\
\hline
1 & patient\_1 & Researcher consent & \{doctor\_1, patient\_1, analyzer\} \\
\hline
2 & analyzer & k-anonymity (k=5) & \{researcher, statistician, public\} \\
\hline
3 & researcher & Aggregates only & \{public\} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\textbf{Breach Investigation}: If patient claims unauthorized data disclosure post-publication, audit trail shows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Patient explicitly authorized analyzer (step 1)
\item Analyzer applied k-anonymity with k=5 (step 2)
\item Researchers published aggregates from 50-patient cohort (step 3)
\item Each step justified and cryptographically signed
\end{enumerate}

Audit proves: data was properly anonymized and aggregated before public release. Patient cannot claim unauthorized disclosure; trail demonstrates due process.

\subsection{Design Choice: Why Decentralized Declassification?}

Project guidelines permit several declassification models. We chose decentralized (owner-authorized) for three reasons:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Scalability}: Hospital with thousands of patients. Centralized administrator bottleneck infeasible. Decentralized model: each patient authorizes own research uses independently.

\item \textbf{Principal Authority}: Myers-Liskov philosophy: principals control policies. Patient owns their medical data; patient should decide research use, not hospital administrator.

\item \textbf{Auditability}: All declassifications logged with authorizing principal and justification. Enables accountability: if breach occurs, trace which principals authorized which data releases.
\end{enumerate}

Alternative (centralized): Requires all declassifications through hospital administrator. Simpler in small systems. But doesn't scale, denies patient autonomy, creates bottleneck.

\subsection{Summary: Declassification as Security Mechanism}

Declassification is legitimate when:
\begin{itemize}
\item Authorized by owner principal
\item Justified by formal condition (k-anonymity, aggregation, differential privacy)
\item Fully audited (logged with timestamp, principal, justification, signature)
\end{itemize}

Declassification enables medical research, data sharing, and multi-stakeholder collaboration while maintaining formal security guarantees and accountability. All three hospital scenario steps satisfy conditions and are auditable.
